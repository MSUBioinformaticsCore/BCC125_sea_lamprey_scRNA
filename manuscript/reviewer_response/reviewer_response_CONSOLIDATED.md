## Methods insert

> **Genome and annotation.** Reads were quantified against *Petromyzon marinus* assembly
> kPetMar1.pri (GCF_010993605.1), using NCBI Annotation Release 100 (submitted 2020-03-20,
> pipeline v8.3; 17,580 protein-coding genes).
>
> **GO annotation.** GO terms were taken from the GO Annotation File distributed with this
> assembly, generated by NCBI on 2023-12-11 using InterProScan v5.64-96.0 and PANTHER v17.0
> against Gene Ontology release 2023-07-27, and comprising 68,145 annotations across 14,859
> genes. NCBI assigns these by two methods: TreeGrafter, which places each protein in the
> phylogenetic tree of its best-matching PANTHER family and propagates terms from annotated
> ancestral nodes (47,575 annotations; Tang et al. 2019); and InterPro2GO, which applies
> curator-assigned entry-to-term mappings to any protein matching the corresponding InterPro
> entry (20,570 annotations; Burge et al. 2012). Annotations were matched to genes using the
> NCBI annotation's own gene symbols as distributed in the GAF. Of the 17,580 protein-coding
> genes in Release 100, 14,859 carry at least one GO term; enrichment used all assayed genes
> (21,967) as the universe, so genes without annotation contribute to the universe without
> being able to appear in any term.
>
> **Gene symbols.** Where the NCBI annotation assigns a symbol we use it; NCBI assigns these
> by pairwise protein alignment combined with local synteny, only where a clear 1:1 relationship
> between loci can be established, with a `-like` suffix marking assignments based on SwissProt
> similarity alone. For genes carrying only LOC identifiers we use the annotation of Wang et al.
> 2024, who assigned symbols by taking the single top-scoring blastp alignment against human
> sequences, with 274 further genes matched against other species. The latter procedure
> establishes homology rather than orthology: it is unidirectional, without reciprocal best-hit,
> synteny or phylogenetic corroboration. Symbols carrying a disambiguating suffix denote
> unresolved one-to-many correspondence. The gene ID to symbol mapping is provided as
> Supplementary Table **[N]**.
>
> **Cell-type assignment.** Cluster identities were assigned from established cell-type markers
> (Supplementary Table S1), cluster-enriched genes (Supplementary Table S4) and GO terms
> overrepresented among those genes (Supplementary Table S5). Assignments were subsequently
> examined at the protein level by immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence, and at the
> transcript level by HCR RNA-FISH, for DDX4, PIWIL1, SYCP3, BOLL, CXCR4, FOXL2, RBPMS, S100A1
> and SOX18. Identities not supported by staining are reported as marker-supported provisional
> transcriptomic identities.
>
> **Sample and batch structure.** Four libraries were generated: two larval replicates
> (C00004336, **[C00003262 or C00004362: Methods and Results disagree, resolve against GEO
> GSE334273]**), submitted 11 July 2023, and one female (C00004337) and one male (C00004374)
> transformer, submitted 8 November 2023. Dissociation was performed at the time of collection
> and library preparation and sequencing were performed by the manufacturer following each
> submission, so developmental stage is confounded with collection date, season and technical
> batch. The two transformer libraries were prepared and sequenced together, so the comparison
> between male and female germ cell trajectories is not confounded with collection time or
> batch, though each sex is represented by a single library.

## Response to comment 1: annotation provenance

> **Comment:** *Cell-type labels and GO interpretations rely substantially on cross-species
> annotation. Please report the annotation database and version, orthology-mapping procedure
> and, where available, the evidence supporting the transferred GO annotations.*
>
> We agree, and should have documented this originally. The Methods now report the annotation
> sources and versions, the mapping procedures, and the evidence available for each class of
> annotation. We address the three points in turn.
>
> **Database and version.** The gene models and the GO terms come from the same assembly but were
> produced at different times, and are versioned separately. The gene models are from NCBI
> Annotation Release 100 (submitted 2020-03-20, pipeline v8.3). The GO terms were added later, on
> 2023-12-11, using InterProScan v5.64-96.0 and PANTHER v17.0 against GO release 2023-07-27. We used the annotation as distributed with the
> assembly, rather than re-running against a current GO release, for reproducibility.
>
> Within InterProScan, GO terms reach a protein by two distinct methods, which we refer to below
> by name. **TreeGrafter** assigns the protein to its best-matching PANTHER protein family,
> places it within that family's phylogenetic tree, and propagates GO terms down from the
> annotated ancestral nodes above its position; terms therefore depend on where within the family
> the protein falls (47,575 of our annotations, 69.8%; Tang et al. 2019). **InterPro2GO** applies
> GO terms that curators have mapped to InterPro entries, so that every protein matching a given
> entry receives the same terms (20,570 annotations, 30.2%; Burge et al. 2012). These are the two
> references cited in the annotation file itself.
>
> **Orthology mapping.** An orthology procedure applies to gene symbols but not to GO
> annotation. For symbols, NCBI assigns orthology by pairwise protein alignment combined with
> local synteny, only where a clear 1:1 locus relationship exists; symbols for LOC genes follow
> Wang et al. 2024, by top-scoring blastp against human, which establishes homology rather than
> orthology and which we now state as a limitation.
>
> For GO annotation there is no orthology-mapping step: neither method identifies an ortholog for
> any lamprey gene, and no annotation is copied from a designated counterpart gene. Cross-species
> information is nonetheless present in both. In the TreeGrafter route, each lamprey protein is
> matched to a protein family in the PANTHER database, families that are defined from the
> sequences of other species; it is then placed within a phylogenetic tree built from those same
> sequences, and inherits GO terms that curators assigned to ancestral positions in that tree on
> the basis of experimental work in characterized species. In the InterPro2GO route, the protein
> family and domain definitions a lamprey protein is matched to, and the GO terms curators have
> assigned to them, are likewise derived from other species. What distinguishes this from ortholog-based transfer is the unit at which
> functional knowledge is applied: a protein family or a phylogenetic node, rather than a
> designated gene in a particular species.
>
> **Supporting evidence.** Every annotation records its method in the reference field, and the
> `with/from` field is populated for all 68,145 annotations, recording the specific PANTHER
> entry (48,431), InterPro entry (31,670) or UniProtKB accession (1,893) from which each term
> derives. Every term used is therefore individually traceable. We have deposited the GAF and
> the per-gene annotation table as Supplementary Data **[N]**.
>
> **What these annotations assert, and their principal limitation.** All 68,145 annotations
> carry evidence code IEA with respect to *P. marinus*: no lamprey gene in this set has been
> experimentally characterized or manually curated.
>
> Both assignment routes rest on curatorial decisions made about protein families as a whole,
> rather than about individual proteins. Under InterPro2GO, each entry in the InterPro database
> defines a protein family or domain, and any given entry is matched by many proteins, in some
> cases thousands, drawn from a wide range of species. A curator assigns a GO term to an entry
> only if that term is true of every protein matching it, because the term is then applied
> automatically to all of them. Under TreeGrafter, a curator assigns a term to an
> ancestral node of a family tree, and every descendant of that node inherits it unless a curator
> has recorded that the function was subsequently lost. In both cases the curator can only draw on those members of the family
> that have been experimentally characterized, and these are predominantly model organisms.
>
> An annotation on a lamprey protein is therefore not a function copied from a
> counterpart gene in any particular species. It is the assertion that a function is conserved
> across a protein family, together with the evidence that the lamprey protein belongs to that
> family. Family membership is the whole of the evidence that the function applies in lamprey,
> across roughly 500 million years of divergence. We have added the following to the Discussion:
>
> > "GO annotations for *P. marinus* are entirely electronic (IEA), derived from assignment of
> > proteins to known protein families rather than from experimental characterization of lamprey
> > genes. Each annotation asserts that a function is conserved throughout a protein family and
> > that the lamprey protein belongs to it; family membership is the entirety of the evidence
> > that the function applies in lamprey. Enrichment results should be read as hypotheses
> > grounded in family membership rather than as evidence of validated function, and are
> > expected to be conservatively biased toward conserved, well-characterized families.
> > Functions that have diverged in the cyclostome lineage, and lamprey-specific genes that
> > cannot be assigned to a recognized family, are systematically invisible to this analysis."

## Response to comment 2: term interpretation and cell-type claims
